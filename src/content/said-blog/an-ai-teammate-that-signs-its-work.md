---
title: "An AI teammate that signs its work"
description: "Claude can sit at your Said desk over MCP — filing cards, leaving comments, and never once acting anonymously."
pubDate: 2026-09-03
---

The problem with most AI-touches-my-stuff features isn't capability. It's
accountability. Something changed, and you can't say what, or when, or by
whose hand.

Said takes the boring, old-fashioned position: everyone at the desk has a
name, including the machines. A Claude session connects over MCP — one
command — and gets real tools: list the boards, search the notes, file a
card, move it, comment in its thread, attach a capture. Every one of those
actions lands in the history signed with the agent's own name, exactly the
way yours land signed with yours.

This turns out to change what you ask for. When an agent's work is
attributable, you can hand it real work. Sweep my notes for loose ends and
file each one as a card, with a reference to the lines it came from. Read
this card's thread and answer the open question in it. Move what's blocked
and say why. Come morning, the board shows what happened — and the case file
shows who did it.

The boundaries are as firm as the signature. An agent can't edit your
markdown — your files aren't its to touch. It can't act unasked. And it
can't hide, because there is no anonymous move in this house.

Collaboration without attribution is just contamination. With it, an AI at
the desk is what it should have been all along: a teammate.
