Every abandoned tracker died the same death: it said one thing, the notes
said another, and one Tuesday you stopped believing it.

## The drift

The tools ask you to move your thinking to them. Import your notes, tag
your notes, restructure your notes — and now you have two sources of truth,
and two is the same as none. The tracker becomes a status theater you
perform for yourself, while the real plan lives in the markdown files you
actually write in.

It doesn't fail loudly. It fails in increments: one item you didn't update
because the real decision happened in a file; one status you stopped
trusting because it disagreed with your own notes; one Tuesday when you
opened the note instead of the tracker and never quite came back.

## The other direction

Said and Done. starts from the other end. You point it at the folder where your notes
already live, and every folder inside becomes a notebook. Nothing is
copied, nothing is converted, nothing is held hostage. The pages are the
moving parts; your files stay the source.

![The Said and Done. record — pages born from real notes, each wearing its status](/said-and-done/shots/board.png)

_The record: pages carry their notebook key for life, and each one points
back into the notes it came from._

There is no import ceremony because there is nothing to import. Make a
folder, and the desk notices. Delete the app and your notes wouldn't
blink — they were never inside it.

## Pages that point at lines

The hinge is references. A page in Said and Done. points at the exact lines of the
note it came from — this decision, that paragraph, those requirements. When
you open the page, the thinking is one keystroke away, read in place from
your own file.

![A page's reference read in place — the markdown lines it points at, rendered inside the desk](/said-and-done/shots/reference.png)

_A reference, read in place. The file stays where it lives; the page just
knows the way._

The record can't drift from the plan, because the record is pinned to the
plan, line by line. When the note changes, the reference follows the file.
When a page is born, Said and Done. even suggests its first reference by scanning
the notebook's own folder — the plan reaches out to the desk before you
ask.

## What the record keeps

And when a page is finished, it files itself away with its history intact —
who moved it, when, and how it ended. Open any page as a case file and its
whole life is on one sheet: the moves, the words, the journeys back from
done.

![A case file — state, lived days, references, and the voices in the thread](/said-and-done/shots/casefile.png)

_The case file: the notes keep the why; the desk keeps the what happened._

That split is the whole philosophy. Your notes were always the source of
truth. Said and Done. just stops pretending otherwise.
