Somewhere in my downloads folder is a graveyard of free trials. Note
apps, task apps, focus apps — each one arrived generous and turned
into a landlord on day fifteen, when the banner slid down: _your trial
has ended_. The features I had built two weeks of habit on grayed out
one by one, like a house where someone walks behind you switching off
the lights. I never once converted. Not because the apps were bad,
but because I cannot respect a gift with a timer in it.

When it came time to price my own desk, I knew what I was not going
to build before I knew anything else. No timer. No gray. No banner
sliding down over your own thoughts.

## A free note taking app with nothing waiting to expire

Said and Done. is free the way a bench in a park is free: not as a
sample of some better bench, but as the actual bench, sit as long as
you like. The free desk is the entire app — every key, every feature,
the search, the phone client, the agents, the backups. There is no
trial clock ticking underneath it and no watermark stamped over it.
Everyone runs the same desk I run.

What free carries is three honest caps, all of them counts: five
Notebooks, ten Chapters, a hundred Pages. Notes are never capped —
the desk will happily sit on a folder of ten thousand files and
search every one of them, because the notes were never mine to meter.
And if you someday sail past a cap, nothing is confiscated: what you
made stays readable, yours, intact. The desk does not take hostages
to make a sale.

I set the caps where a full, real life fits under them. That is not
a teaser strategy; it is the entire product thesis. Most people —
genuinely most, not marketing-most — will use this desk for years
and never owe me anything, and the desk will never once make them
feel like a freeloader about it. No nag screens. No "go premium"
banners. Silence, and a working desk.

## So what is the €4.99 for?

For the day you outgrow the bench — and for something warmer than
that.

One payment of €4.99 lifts every cap, on two devices, forever. No
subscription, nothing renews, nothing to cancel because nothing
recurs. It is priced at a coffee on purpose: low enough that nobody
has to budget for it, real enough that it means something when it
arrives.

Because what it mostly is, truthfully, is a thank-you. The people who
pay are overwhelmingly people who did not have to — people whose five
Notebooks were nowhere near full, who just wanted the solo builder on
the other end to keep building. Some pay through the app, where
Stripe handles the payment and your card details never come anywhere
near me. Some sponsor on GitHub instead. I want to be clear about
how those are received: same hug. There is no premium tier of my
gratitude. A fiver is a stranger saying _keep going_, and it lands
the same way through either door.

I answer to that arrangement differently than I would answer to a
subscription. Recurring revenue quietly rewards captivity — the
metric wants you unable to leave. A one-time thank-you rewards
exactly one thing: the desk being worth thanking. Every incentive I
have points at making it better, none at locking the exits. Your
notes were always plain files on your own disk precisely so the exit
stays open; a business model that needed the exit closed would rot
the product from underneath.

## Guilt-free is a feature

There is a version of free that costs plenty. Free-with-ads costs
your attention. Free-with-tracking costs your privacy on a schedule
you never see. Free-until-we-pivot costs you the migration when the
acquisition lands. Free-as-trial costs you the habit you built before
the gray came. I have paid all four of those bills, and the last one
twice.

This free costs what it says on the label, and it can afford to,
because the desk is cheap to be generous with. There are no servers
holding your notes — they are on your machine. No accounts to
administrate — there are none. No sync fleet, no data team, no ad
inventory. The expensive part of the app is my time, and the
thank-yous cover the part of my time that needs covering. The
economics are small, honest, and boringly stable, which is exactly
how I want the ground under your notes to feel.

So take the bench. Sit for a year. Move your whole thinking life in,
let the desk earn its place, and pay attention to whether anything
gray ever slides down over it. It will not — and if the day comes
when you catch yourself wanting to say thanks anyway, wanting the
thing to keep existing because it quietly holds your days together,
the jar is on the corner of the desk. €4.99, once. Not a toll on the
bridge — a coin tossed to the builder, from the far side, already
across.
