A content calendar in a spreadsheet knows dates. It doesn't know drafts,
references, or the difference between "idea" and "committed."

## Ideas are cheap pages

Every idea gets a backlog page the moment it occurs — one line, `n`, done.
Once a week, promote the few worth writing into to-do. The backlog being
long is a feature: it's a compost heap, not a promise.

## Drafts live as files, pages track their state

Write in markdown in the notebook folder. The page references the draft;
its status carries it from outline to published. When a piece cites an
older note, the reference records the lineage.

![The record reading the pipeline as one account](/said-and-done/shots/dossier.png)

_What shipped, what stalled, what died — the pipeline's honest history._

## Publishing cadence without pressure

The record shows your real cadence, not your aspirational one. Rate the
chapter monthly; when the stars stall, the record shows exactly where —
usually between draft and edit, where content goes to die.
