Solo work has a cruel shape: you are the sales team, the delivery team, and
the person who forgets what was promised. The fix isn't more apps. It's one
desk where a promise can't hide.

## One notebook per client

Make a folder per client inside your working folder — each becomes a
notebook with its own chapters the moment Said and Done. sees it. Client
notes, call summaries, scope docs: they live in that folder as plain
markdown, and every page you file can point at the exact lines that created
the obligation.

![The record carrying real pages, each with references into notes](/said-and-done/shots/board.png)

_Each page wears the file and line range it came from — the promise and its source, joined._

## The week runs backlog to done

New promises land in the backlog with `n`. On Monday, pull the week into
to-do. Work moves forward one chord at a time; nothing lands in done
without passing through your own hands. When a client calls, `/` finds the
page faster than they can finish the sentence — exact words first, meaning
search underneath.

## The paper trail is the product

Six months later, a client asks why something was built the way it was.
Open the page's case file: born date, every status it crossed, the notes it
cites, every voice in its thread. That answer used to take an afternoon of
inbox archaeology. Now it's one sheet.
