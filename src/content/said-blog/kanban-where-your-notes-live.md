---
title: "Why your kanban should live where your notes live"
description: "Every board dies when it drifts from the thinking behind it. Said starts from the folder you already have."
pubDate: 2026-08-20
---

Every abandoned kanban died the same death: the board said one thing, the
notes said another, and one Tuesday you stopped believing the board.

The tools ask you to move your thinking to them. Import your notes, tag your
notes, restructure your notes — and now you have two sources of truth, and
two is the same as none. The board becomes a status theater you perform for
yourself, while the real plan lives in the markdown files you actually write
in.

Said starts from the other end. You point it at the folder where your notes
already live, and every folder inside becomes a project. Nothing is copied,
nothing is converted, nothing is held hostage. The cards are the moving
parts; your files stay the record.

The hinge is references. A card in Said points at the exact lines of the
note it came from — this decision, that paragraph, those requirements. When
you open the card, the thinking is one keystroke away, read in place from
your own file. The board can't drift from the plan, because the board is
pinned to the plan, line by line.

And when a card is finished, it files itself away with its history intact —
who moved it, when, and how it ended. The notes keep the why; the desk keeps
the what happened.

Your notes were always the source of truth. Said just stops pretending
otherwise.
