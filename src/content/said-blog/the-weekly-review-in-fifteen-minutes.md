Weekly reviews die because they're framed as meditation retreats. On this
desk it's a fifteen-minute keyboard pass.

## Minute one to five: walk the record

`j`/`k` through the record. Everything in progress either moves forward,
moves back, or explains itself. The strip at the bottom names every key so
the pass never stalls.

![The record mid-review, cursor on a page](/said-and-done/shots/board-general.png)

_The whole review happens under two hands — no mouse, no menus._

## Minute five to ten: read the week

Scroll the record top-down, freshest first: what was born, what moved,
who said what. This is the part a board alone never gives you — the
account, not just the state.

## Minute ten to fifteen: rate and cut

Rate the chapter — stars are yours alone to give. Then the honest endings:
`abandoned` for the page you've dragged three weeks, `not relevant` for
the one the world solved without you. A desk that only knows "done" makes
you lie to it.
