Weekly reviews die because they're framed as meditation retreats. On this
desk it's a fifteen-minute keyboard pass.

## Minute one to five: walk the lanes

`j`/`k` through each lane. Everything in progress either moves right,
moves back, or explains itself. The strip at the bottom names every key so
the pass never stalls.

![The board mid-review, cursor on a card](/said-and-done/shots/board-general.png)

_The whole review happens under two hands — no mouse, no menus._

## Minute five to ten: read the week

Flip to the dossier. The week reads top-down, freshest first: what was
born, what moved, who said what. This is the part a kanban alone never
gives you — the record, not just the state.

## Minute ten to fifteen: rate and cut

Rate the board — stars are yours alone to give. Then the honest endings:
`abandoned` for the card you've dragged three weeks, `not relevant` for
the one the world solved without you. A desk that only knows "done" makes
you lie to it.
