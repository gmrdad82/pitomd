Every good AI conversation I have starts the same way: with cargo. The
question is the small part — "help me choose the heat pump" is nine
words. The cargo is everything underneath the question: the house
measurements, the electrician's warnings, the quotes, the paragraph
where I argued with myself about radiators. An assistant with the
cargo gives me an answer about _my_ house. An assistant without it
gives me a brochure.

For years, loading the cargo was my job, done by hand, badly. Scroll
the notes, copy a chunk, paste, scroll, copy, paste, forget the one
paragraph that mattered, discover that at the end. The machines kept
getting smarter and my forwarding service stayed a man with a
clipboard. The desk retired the clipboard, in two very different
ways — one for chats, one for agents — and the difference between
them is worth understanding.

## The copy button that packs everything

The chat-sized way is one key. In Said and Done., a Page carries
pins into the exact lines of the notes it depends on — that is
ordinary desk life, the pins accumulating as the topic grows. Press
one key on such a Page and every referenced excerpt is copied at
once: grouped by file, line-numbered, each excerpt labeled with
exactly where it came from. The whole briefing, one block, in your
clipboard.

Then paste it wherever the conversation is happening. Any AI chat,
any provider, the new one that launched on Tuesday — the block is
plain text, and plain text is a passport with every visa. My heat
pump question went into a chat as nine words of asking plus my
complete pinned cargo: measurements, warnings, quotes, the radiator
argument, sources labeled down to the line. The answer that came
back cited _my own notes back to me_ — "given the panel limit you
mention in electrical.md, lines 12 through 15" — and fit my actual
house the way no brochure ever has.

I want to underline what that one key replaces. It is not replacing
typing; it is replacing _curation under pressure_ — the live,
error-prone deciding of what context matters while a chat window
sits open. On the desk, that deciding already happened, calmly,
over weeks, every time I pinned a paragraph to the topic. The
button just cashes it out. Preparation compounds; the clipboard
finally pays interest.

## The door for assistants that fetch

The second way removes even the paste. The desk speaks MCP — the
open protocol AI assistants use to work with tools — and serves it
itself, from inside the running app, to your own machine only.
Flip the toggle in Settings, register the printed one-liner with
your assistant, and from then on the assistant does not need to be
handed the cargo. It asks for it.

A connected assistant can request a Page's references and get back
the very paragraphs the task leans on, line numbers and file
locations included — and it can search the desk with the same
meaning-aware search you use, GPU and all, right through the door. So the
conversation changes shape: instead of me packing a briefing, I say
"research the heat pump task", and the assistant walks to the
topic, reads the exact paragraphs the topic leans on, pulls
whatever else it needs by searching, and returns with the cargo
already digested. Twenty-four tools wait behind that door, but the
soul of it is that one: _give me the lines this task depends on._

Two boundaries make the door trustworthy enough to leave open.
First, it reads notes but cannot write them — no assistant
redecorates your files through it, ever, by construction. Second,
it opens onto your machine alone; nothing is exposed outward, and
closing the desk closes the door. Assistants visit. They do not
move in, and they do not take the notes anywhere, because the
conversation came to the notes instead.

## Pick by the shape of the errand

Two ways, so a quick field guide. The copy button shines when the
thinking is happening _in_ a conversation — exploratory chats,
second opinions, the provider-of-the-week — and you want the topic
in the room without ceremony. The door shines when the errand has a
worker — an assistant meant to go do something with the topic and
report back. I use the button for talking and the door for
delegating, and most weeks I use both before Wednesday.

What they share is the part I actually built: the topic itself,
assembled once, on the desk, as a Page wearing its pins. That is
the quiet reframe hiding under this whole article. The unit you
hand an AI was never the file or the folder — too little, too
much — it was always the _topic_: this intention, plus exactly the
paragraphs it stands on. Keep topics like that as a habit and
the cargo is packed no matter which ship shows up.

The man with the clipboard is retired now. He does not miss the
work. The heat pump, since you are wondering, went in last month —
sized right the first time, argued into place by a machine that had
read my radiator feud down to the line numbers, and filed as done
on the very Page that briefed it.
