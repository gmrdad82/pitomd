People ask the question with a squint, like the number must be hiding
something: why only five euros? The software industry has trained that
squint into all of us — a price this small usually means the real
price is somewhere else, in a subscription behind the curtain or a
data broker behind the app. So let me answer the question with the
books open. The number is small because the free desk is not a demo.
It is the product.

## The ledger: what free actually holds

Free carries exactly three caps, and every one of them is a _count_:
five Notebooks, ten Chapters, a hundred Pages. No clock runs on any
of it — no trial that expires, no feature that grays out on day
fifteen, no watermark. And your notes are never counted at all: the
desk will sit on a folder of ten thousand markdown files and search
every line of them, free, forever, because your files were never
mine to meter.

Everything else is simply included, for everyone:

- The record on every Chapter, a case file behind every Page, and
  the Thoughts and Updates on each — the whole conversation layer.
- The complete keyboard grammar: one mode, no ceremony, every flow
  reachable without a mouse.
- References down to the exact lines: a Page pins any number of
  files, reads them in place, and the pins re-grip when you rewrite.
- Search across titles, bodies, Thoughts and Updates, and the notes themselves —
  including on-device meaning search, running on your own GPU.
- The pocket client: your desk from a phone, served by your own
  machine, over a path you own.
- Agents at the desk over MCP, every action signed with its name.
- Daily backups done properly — a real database snapshot, gzipped,
  fourteen days kept, attachments in a plain tar.
- Every pixel of the same app paying customers run. There is no
  second, nicer binary.

A hundred Pages sounds small until you work the way the desk wants
you to: Pages _resolve_. They land in done — finished, dropped, or
not relevant — and the desk files them away while keeping every one
findable. A living Chapter rarely holds a hundred open intentions,
and if yours genuinely does, you are precisely the person the paid
tier was priced for. Even then, nothing is confiscated at the cap:
what you made stays readable and yours. The sample world never
counts against you either — the guest room is always on the house.

## Where the €4.99 goes

The reason free feels like more than enough is not generosity — it
is that the desk is cheap to be generous with, on purpose. One Rust
program drawing on your own graphics card; one SQLite file in your
home directory; no servers holding your notes, no accounts to
administrate, no telemetry pipeline, because nothing is collecting.
The expensive part of this product is my time, and the fivers cover
the part of my time that needs covering.

And the fivers buy the unglamorous work, which is where the care
actually lives. Pixel measurements instead of eyeballing. The
keyboard law — keys reach only the topmost screen, ever — enforced
in one place so no shortcut can ambush you. Backups that use the
database's own snapshot machinery instead of hoping a file copy
lands between writes. A licence checked offline against a public
key, so no server of mine can ever decide your desk stops working.
Nobody screenshots this kind of work. You feel it, years in, as the
absence of a certain kind of bad day.

## The part that is actually for sale

Because the desk should outlive its launch, and outliving costs a
little. One payment of €4.99 lifts every count, on two devices, for
every 1.x release — no subscription, nothing renews, nothing to
cancel. You had unlimited time to decide before paying, which is
also why a purchase is final: the trial was the product itself, for
as long as you cared to run it.

I could have priced it higher and dressed it in tiers. But the
number is doing philosophical work at five: it is small enough to
be a gesture rather than a decision, and the gesture is the actual
business model. Most people who pay were nowhere near their caps.
They paid the way you drop a coin in a busker's case — not to
start the music, which was already playing, but because they
wanted it to continue.

So: why only five euros? Because the desk was never for sale —
only its future is, and futures come cheap when the present is
already paid for by taste. If the free desk is all you ever need,
good; that was the design. It will keep working, and it will keep
getting better, funded a coffee at a time by people who decided
the thing had said, and done, enough to deserve one.
